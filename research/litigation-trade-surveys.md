# UPI MDR: litigation, traders and survey claims | Edition 03

Evidence cutoff: 15 October 2026, India. The original payment framework was deferred by NPCI on October 13. This bulletin distinguishes court proceedings, reported claims, trade statements and survey predictions.

## Court file and timeline

**16 September 2026:** [LawBeat](https://lawbeat.in/top-stories/pil-before-supreme-court-challenges-centres-move-to-charge-mdr-on-upi-transactions-over-2000-1632215) identified advocate Anjan Datta as petitioner and Ashutosh Dubey as Advocate-on-Record. These sources generally name the bench as CJI Surya Kant, Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana; other contemporaneous reports diverge on the third judge. Do not assert the definitive coram until the signed record is obtained.

An official Supreme Court [28 September cause-list PDF](https://api.sci.gov.in/jonew/cl/2026-09-28/M_J_2.pdf) appears in indexed results with ANJAN DATTA but timed out on retrieval. A **cause list is evidence of scheduled listing**, not independently of the order made. Signed Record of Proceedings remains an active request.

**13 October 2026:** [Paytm signed stock-exchange disclosure](https://paytm.com/document/ir/stock-exchange-submissions/fy2026-27/oct/Disclosure_Reg_30_LODR_13_October_2026.pdf) cites NPCI's decision to postpone implementation to after Diwali, exact date to be notified. This is not a judicial stay. No final determination of the petition's merits is established by the material above.

### Legal conclusions that do not follow

1. Refusal of interim stay does **not** equal a declaration of constitutional validity.
2. A filed PIL does **not** equal a final ruling against the policy.
3. A scheduled case does **not** mean a court granted relief.
4. NPCI's later administrative deferral does **not** retroactively turn the court's refusal to stay into a stay.
5. The 4-week counter-affidavit period is a **reported direction** from the September 28 hearing, not proof that affidavits were actually filed by October 15.

## Consumer and merchant surveys: methodology audit

### Merchant maximum MDR
Publisher: [LocalCircles](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/upi-merchant-survey), 16 Sep 2026. Relevant maximum-fee question **n=32,796** responses, merchants across **242 districts**. Publisher says 48% tier-1, 33% tier-2, 19% tier-3/4 districts; no probability-sampling frame or nonresponse correction has been verified.
- **17%**: willing to bear 0.4% MDR or more under the stated hypothetical question.
- **41%**: would not bear any MDR.
- **9%**: respondents who do not accept UPI; these are not necessarily merchants facing the proposed P2M tariff.
These are answers expressing willingness, **not measured retail acceptance, exit, prices or realised MDR incidence**.

### Consumer conditional response
Publisher: [LocalCircles](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/upi-mdr-consumer-survey), released 2 Oct / consolidated 4 Oct. Publisher describes **67,000+ response records** across 291 districts, not necessarily 67,000 distinct persons.
- Question A on an **explicit surcharge**: **n=31,206** responses, 14% said they would continue paying UPI and bear the extra charge.
- Question B on their **most frequently used payment method if UPI cost extra**: **n=37,654** responses; 76% picked card, cash or bank transfer, 20% picked UPI, 4% unsure. The 76% is not observed transaction migration.
- Publisher's subsequent projected loss of **10% of UPI payment value** and **4% of volume** is a predictive model, not independently validated effect. No policy activation had occurred as of October 15.

### Confounders and identification failures

- The official framework bars merchants from directly passing MDR onto consumers, so a survey scenario of **explicit surcharge** models behavior under a discouraged/prohibited condition. Indirect repricing and steering are different treatment conditions.
- Self-selection by online respondents or merchants can generate systematic response bias, even with tens of thousands of answers.
- Nominal preferences may diverge from actual revealed consumer behavior, especially given rewards, liquidity, merchant acceptance, digital literacy and risk.
- The survey's merchant sample is not a transaction-weighted sample of the **eligible 4% P2M transactions**, so one cannot project fee collections or true pass-through from it.
- Multiple separate question denominators are not additive counts of unique individuals.
- Even if consumers shift from account-funded UPI to credit cards, the merchant might incur a **higher** acceptance cost, limiting the rationality of a cash/card-substitution hypothesis.
