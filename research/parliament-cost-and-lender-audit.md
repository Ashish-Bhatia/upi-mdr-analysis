# Edition 09 — Parliament, the ₹454 crore discrepancy, cost evidence and lender pricing

**Research cutoff: 16 October 2026.** All policy pricing in this paper remains conditional upon implementation after Diwali; no new effective date is officially verified.

## 1. The Ministry's own published aggregates disagree

First-party original source [Lok Sabha Unstarred Question 4111 (18 August 2025)](https://sansad.in/getFile/loksabhaquestions/annex/185/AU4111_DBNB6R.pdf?source=pqals): Minister of State **Pankaj Chaudhary** replying to MP **Anand Bhadauria**:

- Cites the original NPCI August 2019 authorisation of **up to 0.30% MDR on UPI P2M**, subsequently superseded by 2020 zero MDR.
- States approximately **₹8,730 crore** was extended in Government incentive *support* over **FY2021–22 through FY2024–25**.
- Says there is no proposal to impose UPI transaction charges *at that time*, August 2025. It does not speak to a 2026 proposal that did not yet exist.

First-party original source [Ministry/DFS 16 February 2026 Ipsos-study announcement](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2228651):

- States **₹8,276 crore** in scheme disbursements for exactly the same four fiscal labels, broken down FY2021–22 ₹1,389, FY2022–23 ₹2,210, FY2023–24 ₹3,631, FY2024–25 ₹1,046 crore.
- Addition **₹8,276 crore**, internally consistent.

**Difference: ₹454 crore**. A later published total below an earlier approximate statement merits investigation, but is **not** evidence of fraud. Different payout stages, budget authority, cash settlement, scope, net adjustments or data cutoff may account for the gap. These are competing hypotheses, not findings.

**Information request:** For each year obtain scheme type (RuPay Debit vs BHIM-UPI), BE/RE/Actual release, payout authorisation, cash disbursement, reconciliation reversal, claims settled by banks, NPCI accounts, and accounting cutoff. Request a narrative bridge that reconciles 8730→8276 without silently treating the former as a mistaken figure.

## 2. The Standing Committee's own words, finally documented

[Original 44th Standing Committee on Finance Action-Taken Report, August 2026](https://sansad.in/getFile/app/lsscommittee/Finance/18_Finance_44.pdf?source=app), pages 3–6 of printed report / PDF pages 7–10, **reproduces the 32nd report's recommendations** and the DFS reply. The [32nd report catalog](https://elibrary.sansad.in/items/435c4f84-01da-4686-aaa9-edda73d105ab/full) also identifies original PDF 18_Finance_32, not yet fully read.

**Supported conclusions:** The Committee recognised the system cost/incentive gap, supported exploring tiered independent revenue for large-value merchants, maintained protection for smaller merchants and P2P, and asked the Department to minimise permanent pressure on the public exchequer. The DFS answer states **two options** then being explored: (i) restoring MDR for selected high threshold transaction/merchant groups, (ii) tiered incentives to phase out government support in the coming years.

**NOT established:** The reviewed committee paragraphs do not authorise a specific **0.40%** MDR, **₹300** cap, **₹2,000** threshold, **0.28/0.12/0.08%** provider split, or exact implementation date. These originate from later executive/NPCI arrangements. Without full original 32nd report, do not categorically assert absence of any extra passages.

## 3. What does ₹20,700 crore really measure?

DFS's written reply in the original parliamentary PDF is explicit:

> Cost calculation: 15,000 crore P2M transactions × estimated ₹1.38 per transaction = ₹20,700 crore.

The arithmetic is correct. But the per-payment **₹1.38 input** remains an assertion: no comprehensive audited itemised costs or sample of bank participant financial returns accompanies the report. The figure does not distinguish fixed investment from marginal cost, fraud/reversal burdens, attribution to person-to-person traffic, corporate overhead, or overlapping costs among ecosystem participants.

- **Budget provisioning FY2026–27**: ₹2,000 crore.
- **Claimed operating cost**: ₹20,700 crore.
- **Ratio**: ~9.66%.
- Committee's earlier cited share '11% of actual costs' and a separate '14% of potential MDR collections' are not independent measurements. They may use different denominators or earlier estimates. Publish the exact underlying figures rather than silently forcing them into consistency.

A defensible MDR requires separating marginal per-payment servicing cost from capacity/risk fixed expense, determining whether incidence ought to be transactional, taxpayer funded or mixed, and testing net welfare and competition consequences. The parliamentary document adds provenance, not an independent cost audit.

## 4. What the new rate might mean for lenders

[Vinod Kothari Consultants' 20-page original analysis](https://vinodkothari.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/MDR-on-UPI-_-As-applicable-on-Lenders.pdf) applies proposed UPI merchant fees to lenders:
- Ordinary **manual one-off ₹10,000 EMI collection** treated as standard UPI P2M could have **₹40 pre-tax merchant MDR**.
- Eligible **Debt Collection** Industry Program category, MCC described as **7322 or 5413** in specialist analysis, could attract **flat ₹5 per qualifying P2M transaction**, not standard 0.40%.
- **AutoPay recurring mandate** is excluded by official NPCI FAQ Q22 and an apparent circular note; an ad-hoc retry after mandate failure may be treated differently if it becomes a separate manual payment.
- A loan disbursement is not automatically P2M merchant acceptance and cannot be charged simply because money passes through UPI.

**Consultant's illustrative IRR:** a one-year ₹1,00,000 nominal 18% loan with roughly ₹9,168 monthly EMIs, subject to 0.40% MDR on each manual EMI, is modeled as reducing effective lender IRR by about 77 basis points to 17.23%. This uses hypothetical payment mode, classification and rate. It does not describe actual post-implementation bank portfolio yields.

**Merchant refunds:** the consultant asserts MDR may be kept despite a later refund because settlement service has been provided. This is a commercial interpretation; the precise binding refund and chargeback rule must be checked in NPCI member circulars, acquiring-bank contracts and PSP settlement records. Do **not** promote it to proven national law.
