# Edition 04 — UPI MDR public funding and operating-rule audit
**Cutoff:** 15 October 2026. This publication distinguishes *documentary findings* from *operational questions* and *hypothetical arithmetic*.

## Finding A: the ₹2,000 crore FY2026–27 budget entry
The Ministry of Finance's [Output-Outcome Monitoring Framework 2026–27](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/OutcomeBudgetE2026_2027.pdf), Department of Financial Services, Demand No. 32, printed page 115 (PDF page 123), provides:
- **₹2,000 crore** financial outlay for *Incentive Scheme for Promotion of RuPay Debit Cards and low-value BHIM-UPI (P2M)*.
- **10%** targeted increase in UPI acceptance infrastructure.
- **18,187 crore** target merchant BHIM-UPI P2M transactions.
- **25%** targeted BHIM-UPI transaction growth versus FY2025–26.

The budget table does **not** establish: actually disbursed FY2026–27 funds; who received the allocation; terms after future MDR commencement; whether the new merchant fund supplants, complements or overlaps the public subsidy; or measurable impacts attributable to the programme.

**Comparison to the original 2025 Cabinet scheme:** [PIB Cabinet release dated 19 March 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2112771) approved estimated ₹1,500 crore for FY2024–25 and specified **0.15%** incentive on eligible ≤₹2,000 small-merchant transactions. Acquiring banks received **80% unconditionally**, **10% contingent on technical decline below 0.75%**, **10% contingent on uptime above 99.5%**. Historical FY2021–22/23/24 amounts ₹957/1,802/3,268 crore were BHIM-UPI incentive *payouts*. These figures are distinct from the FY2026–27 new *outlay*.

**Policy question:** What is the net incremental funding supplied by MDR, after adjusting for continuing subsidies and incremental cost of implementation? Without the actual budget-release records, MDR collections by participant and audited annual UPI costs, it is not computable.

## Finding B: 2020 law contained refund enforcement
Original Department of Revenue/CBDT [Circular No. 16/2020](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/circular-no.-16/2020-imposition-of-charge-on-the-prescribed-electronic-modes-under-section-269su-of-the-income-tax-act-1961), dated 30 August 2020, notes alleged UPI charges after January 1, 2020 in breach of then-applicable PSS Act Section 10A and Income-tax Act Section 269SU. It directs banks to refund fees collected from January 1, 2020 on specified modes and cites penalties under IT Act Section 271DB and PSS Act Section 26.

Interpretation: zero-MDR was not merely a voluntary industry norm. However, the 2026 framework followed a later legislative amendment. The 2020 circular does not itself decide the legality of post-amendment charges.

## Finding C: capital-market 0.02% fee has a distribution ambiguity
[Four-page third-party-hosted copy of NPCI OC-237](https://www.madhyabharatparidrishya.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/OC_237_MDR-2026.pdf), page 1:
- Paragraph 3 standard *gross* MDR up to **0.40%** merchant → acquirer and sequential transfer rates **0.28%** acquirer → issuer; **0.12%** issuer → payer PSP; **0.08%** payer PSP → app.
- Paragraph 4 capital-market MDR **0.02%** (₹300 cap) says distribution to participants is as prescribed in paragraph 3.

The standard chain is internally feasible because each party pays an *onward* rate lower than or equal to its incoming rate, leaving residual retained funds. The capital-market literal cross-reference is not feasible: 0.28% would exceed the entire 0.02% gross charge by a factor of **14**. At ₹10,000, capital-market gross ₹2 versus standard-table interchange ₹28. So the actual allocation must differ or require other funding; this source does not explain it.

**Required verification:** authenticated NPCI circular or erratum; bank clearing/settlement specification; merchant acquisition contract tariff schedules; calculation example for a mutual-fund transaction. We mark it *unresolved*, not an allegation of actual overcharging.

## Finding D: mandates and GST tax multiplier are special cases
The same circular page 1 note (b) excludes *mandate-related pricing* from the new circular. [Official NPCI FAQ](https://www.npci.org.in/uploads/FA_Qs_Merchant_Discount_Rate_MDR_on_Select_UPI_P2_M_Transactions_58dba1d39e.pdf), Q22, similarly says recurring UPI AutoPay payments do not attract the prescribed new MDR.

The circular copy's notes say quoted rates are exclusive of GST and refer to a **1.1092 multiplier**. The quoted factor is not explained within the four pages and should not be equated to standard 18% tax or applied by default. The website's calculator models only nominal *pre-tax* fee.

## Finding E: a 2016 RBI transparency precedent, not a 2026 UPI directive
[RBI Circular dated 1 September 2016](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=10591) instructed card acquirers to unbundle MDR by debit, credit and prepaid product, disclose the fee to merchants and educate them. It was aimed at *card transactions*, not the proposed 2026 account-funded UPI charge; the analogy establishes a potential disclosure standard, not that this circular directly controls OC-237.
